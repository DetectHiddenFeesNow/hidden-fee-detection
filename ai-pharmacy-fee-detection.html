What AI Pharmacy Fee Detection Identifies

Brand-Name Pricing for Generic Drugs

Patients are sometimes charged brand-name prices even when generic equivalents are available and dispensed. AI detection identifies National Drug Code mismatches where the billed NDC corresponds to a brand-name drug but the patient received the generic, therapeutic equivalence codes that indicate a generic was dispensed but pricing reflects the brand rate, and DAW (Dispense As Written) codes that override generic substitution without medical necessity. The average overcharge for brand-name pricing on a generic drug is $80 to $200 per prescription. Patients taking multiple medications can face hundreds of dollars in unnecessary charges per month.

Pharmacy Benefit Manager Spread Pricing

PBMs negotiate drug prices with pharmacies then charge insurance plans a higher amount, keeping the difference as profit. AI detection identifies the PBM's reimbursement rate to the pharmacy compared to the amount billed to the insurance plan, ingredient cost adjustments that reduce pharmacy payments without reducing patient costs, and direct and indirect remuneration fees that claw back payments from pharmacies after the point of sale. Spread pricing adds $10 to $50 per prescription in hidden costs that are passed to patients through higher premiums and copays.

Clawback Fees

Insurance clawbacks occur when the insurance company recovers part of the drug cost from the pharmacy after the patient has paid their copay. AI detection identifies copay amounts that exceed the actual cost of the medication, accumulator adjustment programs where copay assistance from manufacturers does not count toward the patient's deductible, and copay cards that are rejected at the pharmacy counter because the insurance processor has already applied a clawback. Clawback fees add $20 to $100 or more per prescription to patient costs.

90-Day Supply Pricing Manipulation

90-day supplies should cost approximately 2 to 2.5 times the 30-day price. AI detection identifies 90-day supply pricing at 3 times or more the 30-day rate, maintenance medication pricing that penalizes patients for using mail-order or automatic refill programs, and split-fill billing where a 90-day supply is billed as three separate 30-day prescriptions each with a separate copay. The overcharge for manipulated 90-day pricing averages $50 to $150 per medication per fill.

Therapeutic Substitution Markups

Therapeutic substitution occurs when a pharmacy dispenses a different drug in the same therapeutic class. AI detection identifies substitutions within the same drug class that are priced significantly higher than the prescribed medication, therapeutic interchange programs that switch patients to more expensive drugs without disclosure, and prior authorization requirements that force patients toward more expensive alternatives. Therapeutic substitution overcharges average $100 to $300 per prescription.

Mail-Order Pharmacy Overcharges

Mail-order pharmacies charge shipping, handling, and processing fees. AI detection identifies shipping fees that exceed actual delivery costs, automatic refill programs that ship refills without authorization, and auto-ship renewal fees for standing orders. Mail-order pharmacy fees add $60 to $180 per year to medication costs.

Compounding Pharmacy Markups

Compounded medications are custom-mixed and priced individually. AI detection identifies compounding fees that exceed the cost of ingredients by 500 to 1,000 percent, preparation fees that duplicate the compounding charge, and consultation fees for discussing the prescription with the pharmacist. Compounding markups average $50 to $200 per prescription beyond reasonable ingredient costs.

Why Pharmacy Hidden Fees Are a Pervasive Problem

The US prescription drug market exceeds $500 billion annually with an estimated 10 to 15 percent representing overcharges, billing errors, and PBM price manipulation. The complexity of pharmacy pricing, involving multiple intermediaries including drug manufacturers, wholesale distributors, pharmacy benefit managers, insurance plans, and retail pharmacies, creates a system where patients have no visibility into the actual cost of their medications. A 2025 report by the Federal Trade Commission found that the three largest PBMs processed 80 percent of all prescription claims in the United States.

Types of Pharmacy Documents Reviewed

Prescription Receipts

Pharmacy receipts show the drug name, quantity, price, insurance payment, and patient responsibility. AI reviews each receipt for NDC accuracy, DAW code appropriateness, quantity pricing correctness, and copay amount reasonableness.

Explanation of Benefits Forms

Prescription drug EOB forms from insurance companies itemize the amount billed, plan payment, and patient responsibility. AI identifies discrepancies between the EOB and the pharmacy receipt indicating clawbacks or spread pricing.

PBM Statements

Pharmacy benefit manager statements show drug pricing, rebates, and fees. AI detects spread pricing between the PBM's reimbursement to pharmacies and the amount billed to the plan.

Mail-Order Pharmacy Contracts

Mail-order agreements specify shipping terms, auto-refill programs, and pricing guarantees. AI detects shipping fee inflation and unauthorized automatic shipments.
