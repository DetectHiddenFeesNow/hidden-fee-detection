Why Understanding Preneed Funeral Contracts Matters

Preneed funeral contracts allow individuals to prepay for funeral goods and services, locking in current prices and relieving family members of making arrangements during a difficult time. While these contracts can provide peace of mind, they also involve complex terms and fee structures that may not be immediately apparent. The Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule requires funeral homes to provide itemized pricing, but preneed contract terms can vary significantly by state and provider. Understanding the fee structure, portability, and cancellation terms helps consumers make informed decisions about whether a preneed contract is appropriate for their situation.

Common Hidden Fees in Preneed Funeral Contracts

Merchandise and Service Price Inflation

Some preneed contracts price merchandise and services at amounts above the funeral home's current general price list. While inflation protection is one of the stated benefits of preneed contracts, the starting price should be based on current retail prices. Consumers who sign a preneed contract with inflated starting prices may not receive the full value of their prepayment. Comparing the contract prices against the funeral home's general price list can help identify this issue.

Policy Exclusions and Coverage Gaps

Burial insurance policies and preneed contracts may exclude certain goods or services. Common exclusions include cemetery costs, opening and closing fees, monument or marker expenses, and obituary publication charges. Family members may be responsible for these excluded costs in addition to the prepaid amount. Reviewing the contract for specific exclusions helps prevent unexpected expenses at the time of need.

Portability and Transfer Limitations

Preneed contracts are typically tied to a specific funeral home. If the buyer moves to another state or wants to use a different funeral home, the contract may be difficult to transfer. Some contracts allow transfer only to affiliated locations, while others require cancellation and refund rather than transfer. Cancellation may result in forfeiture of some fees or reduced refunds depending on state law and contract terms.

Administrative and Cancellation Fees

Preneed contracts may include administrative fees for setting up and maintaining the contract, periodic statement fees, and cancellation or surrender fees. These fees can reduce the amount available for funeral goods and services. State laws regulate permissible fees and cancellation refund amounts in many jurisdictions, but requirements vary.

Red Flags in Preneed Funeral Contracts

  • Contract prices that exceed the funeral home's current general price list
  • Exclusions for commonly needed services or merchandise
  • Limited portability or transfer options
  • Cancellation penalties that forfeit a significant portion of the prepaid amount
  • Administrative fees that reduce the principal balance
  • Contracts that do not clearly specify which goods and services are covered

Frequently Asked Questions

Are preneed funeral contracts a good idea?

Preneed contracts can provide peace of mind and lock in current prices, but they are not right for everyone. Factors to consider include whether you plan to stay in the same area, whether the contract covers the goods and services you want, and whether the pricing is fair. Comparing the contract against the funeral home's current price list and reviewing the terms carefully helps in making an informed decision.

Can I cancel a preneed funeral contract?

Most preneed contracts allow cancellation, but the refund amount depends on state law and contract terms. Some states require full refunds of the prepaid amount, while others allow deductions for administrative fees or the cost of insurance. The contract should specify cancellation terms. Requirements vary by jurisdiction.

What happens if I move to another state?

Portability depends on the contract terms. Some contracts allow transfer to another funeral home within the same network. Others may require cancellation and refund if you move outside the service area. Transferring a preneed contract across state lines can be complicated because funeral regulations vary by state. Review the portability clause before signing.

Does the Funeral Rule apply to preneed contracts?

The FTC's Funeral Rule requires funeral homes to provide a general price list and itemized pricing. Preneed contracts are subject to the Funeral Rule, but state laws also regulate preneed funeral contracts in many jurisdictions. The specific requirements that apply depend on the state where the contract is signed.
