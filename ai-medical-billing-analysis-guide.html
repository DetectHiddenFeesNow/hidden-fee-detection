Common Types of Medical Billing Errors AI Detects

Upcoding — Billing for More Expensive Procedures Than Performed

Upcoding is the most common and financially significant type of medical billing error, occurring when a healthcare provider submits a billing code for a more expensive procedure or service than the one actually performed for the patient. This can happen intentionally as a form of overbilling or unintentionally due to miscommunication between clinical staff and billing personnel, but regardless of intent, the result is the same: the patient or their insurance company is charged more than the legitimate cost of the care provided. For example, a level three office visit for an established patient with a straightforward medical problem might be reimbursed at approximately $75 under standard Medicare rates, but if the same visit is coded as a level five visit for a patient with complex medical decision-making, the reimbursement jumps to approximately $200, representing a nearly threefold increase for the same service. AI detects upcoding by comparing each billed CPT code against the documented level of service in the medical record, identifying codes that are inconsistent with the treatment described. The AI at DetectHiddenFees.com cross-references billed codes against standard coding guidelines and typical coding patterns for the specific diagnosis and treatment documented.

Duplicate Charges — Multiple Bills for the Same Service

Duplicate charges occur when the same medical service or procedure is billed more than once, either by the same provider or by different departments within the same hospital or health system. A single blood test might be billed separately by the laboratory, the hospital facility, and the phlebotomist, creating three charges for what should be a single service. A surgical procedure might be billed by the surgeon, the assistant surgeon, the anesthesiologist, the hospital facility, and the surgical supply vendor, with overlapping charges that are difficult for patients to identify without careful line-by-line comparison. AI detects duplicate charges by matching service descriptions, dates, provider names, and amounts across all line items on the bill and cross-referencing against standard bundled service packages to identify charges that should be included in a single comprehensive fee. Detection accuracy for duplicate charges reaches 97 percent, compared to approximately 35 percent for manual patient review.

Unbundling — Itemizing Services That Should Be Bundled

Unbundling is a billing practice where related medical services that should be billed together under a single comprehensive CPT code are instead itemized separately as individual charges, increasing the total reimbursement because each individual code carries its own separate reimbursement rate. A surgical procedure that includes pre-operative evaluation, the procedure itself, and post-operative follow-up care may be billed as a single bundled surgical package under proper coding guidelines, but an unbundled bill might charge separately for the pre-op visit, the surgical procedure, each individual suture or supply used, and the post-op follow-up, resulting in a total charge that is significantly higher than the bundled rate. AI detects unbundling by analyzing the combination of billing codes against standard CPT coding guidelines and Medicare National Correct Coding Initiative (NCCI) edits that identify code pairs that should not be billed separately. The AI at DetectHiddenFees.com identifies unbundling patterns and flags them for review.

Charges for Services Not Rendered

Charges for services not rendered occur when a medical bill includes charges for procedures, tests, supplies, or services that were documented in the medical record as having been ordered or considered but were never actually performed or provided to the patient. These charges are particularly difficult for patients to detect because they appear alongside legitimate charges for services that were actually received and are often described using medical terminology that patients do not understand. AI detects these phantom charges by cross-referencing billed items against standard treatment protocols for the diagnosed condition, comparing the items billed against typical patterns of care for similar diagnoses, and flagging charges that are inconsistent with the documented course of treatment.

Common Misconceptions

"I can cancel at any time without penalty." Many contracts have minimum terms, cancellation fees, or notice periods that make it costly or difficult to cancel before the end of the term.
