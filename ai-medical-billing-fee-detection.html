What AI Medical Fee Detection Identifies

Upcoding Errors

Hospitals and providers bill using CPT and ICD-10 codes that determine reimbursement. AI detects evaluation and management visits coded at higher levels than supported by the documentation, surgical procedures coded with more complex codes than performed, and modifier misuse where codes are appended incorrectly to justify higher reimbursement.

Duplicate Charges

Duplicate charges are the most common medical billing error. AI detects the same procedure code appearing multiple times on the same bill, daily charges for supplies that should be one-time charges, and pharmacy charges where a single medication administration is billed as both a pharmacy charge and a nursing charge.

Unbundled Services

Medical procedures are often bundled into a single comprehensive code. AI detects incidental procedures that are billed separately when they should be included in the primary procedure code, supplies that are itemized individually when they are included in the procedure charge, and pre-operative and post-operative care billed separately from the surgical fee.

Facility and Professional Fee Duplication

Hospital outpatient visits generate both a facility fee and a professional fee. AI detects facility fees charged for visits to hospital-owned clinics that should be billed as standard office visits, facility fees that exceed the Medicare-approved amount by unreasonable margins, and duplicate facility and professional fees for the same service.

Out-of-Network Billing Errors

Patients who receive care at in-network facilities may still be treated by out-of-network providers. AI detects balance bills for out-of-network providers when the patient was treated at an in-network facility without being informed of the out-of-network provider, out-of-network charges that exceed the No Surprises Act protections, and emergency room physician charges that should be covered at the in-network rate under federal law.

Observation Status vs. Inpatient Classification

Patients placed in observation status are billed differently than admitted inpatients. AI detects observation stays exceeding 48 hours that should qualify for inpatient admission under Medicare rules, observation status for patients with conditions that clearly meet inpatient criteria, and skilled nursing facility bills where the preceding hospital stay did not meet the 3-day inpatient requirement.

Pharmacy and Supply Markups

Hospitals mark up medications and supplies significantly. AI detects pharmacy charges at 5 to 20 times the acquisition cost, single-use device fees for items that should be included in the procedure charge, and supply charges for items that were not actually used during the patient's stay.
